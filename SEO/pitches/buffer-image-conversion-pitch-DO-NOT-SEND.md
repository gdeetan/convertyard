## Why this pitch shouldn't be sent as drafted

### Problem 1: Buffer's guest submissions are closed

From https://buffer.com/resources/write-for-us/:
> *"⚠️ Applications are currently closed – check back in periodically for updates!"*

### Problem 2: The pitch is exactly what their "who shouldn't apply" list rules out

Buffer's own guest post program page lists — verbatim:
> *"This isn't a fit if you're:*
> - *A freelance writer pitching unrelated blog topics*
> - *Looking for a paid blogging gig or a regular contributor role*
> - *Hoping to link-build or promote unrelated products/services"*

An image-conversion pitch with ConvertYard as the "part of the workflow" is exactly what those bullets describe. Sending it to a small content team that explicitly said no to this pattern doesn't just get rejected — it makes future applications harder.

### Problem 3: Category mismatch

Buffer's blog covers social media strategy, growth systems, and community building — Shivani's image-sizes guide is one of the few pieces on their site about actual image mechanics, and even it's a sizes reference, not a workflow guide. Image-format conversion is 2-3 conceptual jumps away from Buffer's editorial focus.

### Problem 4: The pitch's factual claim is likely wrong

The draft says "most social platforms either reject [HEIC] outright or convert badly." As of 2026, Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok, and Threads all auto-convert HEIC on upload without visible degradation. Bluesky handles it via image auto-processing (per Shivani's article: *"images are processed and resized by the platform after upload"*). The HEIC-upload wall does exist for some workflows (bulk desktop uploaders, older schedulers, some CMS integrations) but not for the standard "post from phone or web" case. If Buffer's team fact-checks the pitch, they'll find the premise doesn't hold.

## Honest recommendation

**Skip Buffer entirely for the ConvertYard pitch batch.** Better use of the same hour:

- **Later, Sprout Social, or Hootsuite** — competitor scheduling tools with more open guest-post programs. If your angle is "image workflow for social managers," these fit better than Buffer's growth-story-focused model.
- **Actual photographer/creator publications** you've already pitched (Shutterbug, Phoblographer) — real fit.
- **A self-published Hashnode article** on "preparing iPhone photos for social upload" — you can build this out as one of the fCC contributor samples and it becomes distributable content for your own channels.

## If you insist on Buffer eventually — right approach

Wait until the next cohort application window opens (Buffer said they'd open windows periodically). Then, when open, submit a pitch that matches their actual model:

**Working title:** *"The System I Used to Build a Free Web Tool to 10,000 Users in Six Months (Without a Marketing Budget)"*

- Personal growth story about building and shipping ConvertYard
- Real metrics, real timeline, real screenshots
- Buffer positioned as one tool in the system (if you actually used it) — not the pitch's subject
- Zero mention of image conversion as a topic; ConvertYard is only mentioned as the thing you built

That's what their program is designed to publish. It also gets you a Buffer byline, a dofollow bio link to convertyard.com, and reach to their audience — all without triggering the "who shouldn't apply" filter.

**When to check:** their program has been on-and-off. Set a reminder to check https://buffer.com/resources/write-for-us/ every 6-8 weeks.
