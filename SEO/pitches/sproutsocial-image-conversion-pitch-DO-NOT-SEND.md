# Sprout Social pitch — DO NOT SEND

**Reference article:** "Always Up-To-Date Guide to Social Media Image Sizes"
**URL:** https://sproutsocial.com/insights/social-media-image-sizes-guide/
**Author:** Mary Keutelian (in-house Sprout Social content team)
**Last updated:** May 11, 2026
**Length:** long-form guide, 10 platforms including Google Business Profile

## Why this pitch shouldn't be sent

### Problem 1: No submission channel exists

All standard paths return 404:
- `/insights/write-for-us/` → 404
- `/insights/contribute/` → 404
- `/insights/editorial-guidelines/` → 404
- `/insights/guest-post/` → 404
- `/insights/contact/` → redirects to /insights/
- `/contact/` → 404
- No archived write-for-us record on Wayback

Sprout Social does not accept unsolicited guest submissions. Their blog is nearly 100% in-house, with rare invited bylines from well-known industry practitioners. A cold pitch from an unknown contributor with a tool to promote will not clear their filters — there's no filter to clear, because there's no intake channel.

### Problem 2: Sprout has its own competing image tool in the article

Mary's guide prominently promotes **Landscape** — Sprout's own free image resizing tool — linked twice with an animated GIF demo. Sprout's business model on this article is: reader lands on the guide → sees Landscape → tries Landscape → enters the Sprout free trial funnel.

Adding a link to ConvertYard as a "step before your guide" pulls readers *away* from that funnel, toward a third-party tool. There is zero business incentive for Sprout to make that edit, regardless of how the pitch is written.

### Problem 3: Category mismatch (same as Buffer)

Sprout's editorial focus is social strategy, community, platform features, agency workflows, and reporting. Image format conversion is 2-3 conceptual jumps from that focus.

### Problem 4: The pitch premise is likely overstated

- Instagram, Facebook, LinkedIn, TikTok, and Threads all auto-convert HEIC uploads in 2026
- Sprout Social itself likely accepts HEIC uploads via its media upload API (couldn't fully verify — their support docs 403'd — but their general approach is server-side format normalization on ingest)
- The "social manager receives 40 iPhone HEICs and can't proceed" case is real but far narrower than the pitch implies
- If Sprout's team fact-checks the pitch's premise against their own supported-file-types page, the "missing first step" framing won't hold

## Honest recommendation

**Skip Sprout Social entirely for the ConvertYard pitch batch.** This one is even less winnable than Buffer:
- Buffer at least has a submission program (currently closed but real)
- Sprout has no program, no channel, no email
- Sprout's article promotes their own image tool that ConvertYard would compete with
- Sprout's in-house content model is one of the tightest in social SaaS

## Alternate approach if you want a social media publication placement

Neither Buffer nor Sprout is the right fit. Better-fit publications that accept guest contributions on adjacent topics:

- **Search Engine Journal** — accepts guest contributions, covers technical SEO topics including image optimization. Angle: "How Serving HEIC vs. WebP Affects Your Site's Core Web Vitals" (technical SEO angle for the same tool).
- **Marketing Land / MarTech** — occasionally publishes practitioner pieces on operational workflow topics.
- **Content Marketing Institute** — has a guest contributor program (though selective) for practitioner content.
- **Later.com blog** — social scheduler like Sprout/Buffer, historically more open to contributor content.
- **Hashnode / dev.to / Medium** — self-publish the workflow piece, get it distributed, use it as a portfolio sample for future outreach.

Time you'd have spent on the Sprout pitch is better spent on one of these, or on the Cloudways/iGeeksBlog/Windows Latest/Shutterbug quartet that will actually convert.
