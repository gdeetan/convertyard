Subject: What your Troy Carter episode didn't get to

Dan,

Listened to the Troy Carter episode — his line about Suno being more
consequential than the phonograph is the kind of take that either ages
terribly or becomes obvious. I think he's right.

One thing you didn't get to, which I keep seeing come up in producer
circles: the file problem. Suno and similar tools spit out MP3s and
low-bitrate WAVs. Getting those into a deliverable format for distribution
or a real session means running them through something — and most people
are using random cloud converters because they're fast.

That gets messy fast when the track is unreleased. Files going through
third-party servers, no idea where they're stored, and no one thinks about
it until there's a leak problem.

I built ConvertYard to handle this locally — everything converts in the
browser, nothing hits a server. Built it partly because this kept coming up.
There's a piece here: AI created a new layer of production workflow that
the industry hasn't caught up to, and it's quietly a rights/security problem.

Happy to send an outline if this fits something you're planning.
