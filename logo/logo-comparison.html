ConvertYard Logo Directions

Three distinct approaches. Pick one (or mix elements) and we'll refine into production files.

Direction A

Stacks — Workshop / Crates

Three offset rectangles suggest stacks of files or crates in a yard. Most literal to the "yard" metaphor. Communicates: batch, organized, workshop.

Full lockup
Mark only (favicon size)
At 32px (browser tab)

Strengths

  • Direct connection to "yard" + "batch" wedge
  • Reads clearly at small sizes
  • Unique among converter sites
  • Easy to animate (stacks growing)

Considerations

  • Could be mistaken for a logistics brand
  • Less obviously "conversion"-coded
Direction B

Convert Arrow — Transformation

A document silhouette with an arrow piercing through it. Literally depicts file transformation. Communicates: conversion, action, file-focused.

Full lockup
Mark only
At 32px

Strengths

  • Immediately communicates "convert"
  • File-icon shape is universally understood
  • Clean and tool-coded

Considerations

  • Slightly busier at small sizes
  • Less unique — many tool brands use file icons
  • Doesn't lean into "yard" specifically
Direction C

Wordmark — Pure Typography

All lowercase, modern, with a brand-colored dot separator. Mark is a "cy" monogram in a rounded square for favicon use only. Communicates: confident, technical, minimal.

Full lockup
Favicon mark
At 32px

Strengths

  • Most modern / startup-feeling
  • Lowest risk — wordmarks age well
  • Fastest to refine and ship
  • Easy to redesign mark later

Considerations

  • Less distinctive at a glance
  • Relies entirely on type quality
  • No visual story about "yard" or "batch"

My recommendation

Go with Direction A (Stacks). It's the only one that visually tells your story — stacked files in a yard, batch processing — without explanation. The mark survives at 16px (favicon), animates beautifully (stacks growing as you convert), and nothing else in the converter space looks like this. Direction C is the safe second choice if you want pure simplicity. Direction B is the weakest because every file-conversion site uses a file-icon-with-arrow somewhere.
